League Rules

Market Cross Invitation Darts League — grouped by topic, numbered section.rule (e.g. 5.3).

1. The League

1.1The league shall be called the Market Cross Invitation Darts League.
1.2The league consists of two divisions, Division 1 and Division 2.
1.3Any team may be invited into the league.

2. Committee

2.1The committee shall be made up of a Chairman, Secretary, and Treasurer.
2.2All decisions made by the committee are final.
2.3All teams must have a member present at any meeting called by the committee.
2.4Failure to comply with any rule made by the committee may lead to a team's expulsion from the league, at the committee's sole discretion.

3. Fees

3.1All fees (£30.00 per team) must be paid to the treasurer before the start of the season.
3.2The secretary's honorarium is £70 per year, to cover all expenses.

4. Player Eligibility

4.1No player may play for more than one team in a season.
4.2A player who becomes landlord/landlady of another team within the league — or vice versa — may only represent the new team with the committee's approval.

5. Match Night

5.1Results are entered directly via the Captain Portal on the league website on the night of the match. Captains should also confirm the result in the captains' WhatsApp group.
5.2All teams must be present by 8.15pm for the draw for order of play, which will commence at 8.30pm on a standard dartboard.
5.3To avoid cancelling games, a team may play a match with 5 players — one pair is automatically forfeited to the opposing team, and the opposing captain may draw one of their players at random to play the singles twice.
5.4Games should only be cancelled in exceptional circumstances and with reasonable notice. The cancelling captain must let the opposing captain know as soon as possible, as well as the League Secretary.
5.5If a game is postponed, it should be rearranged for the next available catch-up week (three are set aside each season) or an alternative weekday. If the two captains can't agree a date, the committee will make the final decision.
5.6Captains are asked to be reasonably flexible where a player is late due to being a member of the emergency services, or due to work shifts. That player must be in attendance before the start of the 5th game in the singles, or the 2nd game in the doubles.
5.7If a team fails to complete a fixture, the opposition is awarded the match, with the result recorded as 5–0.

6. Game Format & Scoring

6.1Each match consists of 3 doubles games and 6 singles games.
6.2Doubles are best of 3 legs, 501, double start and double finish, and are played first.
6.3Singles are best of 3 legs, 501, straight start and double finish.
6.4The outer bull counts as 25, the inner bull as 50 — the inner bull also counts as a double for match purposes.
6.5Each game starts with both players bulling up. The winner throws first in leg 1 and leg 3 (if the game goes to a decider); the other player throws first in leg 2.
6.6One point is awarded for each game won — 9 points are available per fixture.

7. Equipment

7.1Boards are set at 5ft 8" to the centre of the bull. The oche is placed 7ft 9¼" from the board, measured horizontally along the floor (9ft 7½" diagonally, bull to oche), and must be at least 2ft long and 2" square. The oche must stay fixed in position for the duration of the match.

8. Standings & Tie-Breaks

8.1League and Cup tables are decided by points (games won, out of 9 per fixture). Where two or more teams finish level, position is decided in this order:
  1. Head-to-head result, where the tied teams have played each other
  2. Total number of matches won
  3. Leg difference (legs won minus legs lost, across every game played)

9. Cup Competition

9.1All teams are entered into the cup, which runs as a group stage followed by two knockout competitions — the Knock Out Cup and the Subsidiary Cup.
9.2Teams are randomly drawn at the AGM and entered into a predetermined template, which decides each team's 5 group-stage opponents — each team ends up with either 3 home and 2 away fixtures or 2 home and 3 away, the luck of the draw. Cup fixtures are played to the same format as the league (see Game Format & Scoring above), and every result feeds into a single combined cup table.
9.3Once the group stage is complete, the top 4 teams in the cup table enter the Knock Out Cup, and 5th–8th enter the Subsidiary Cup. Semi-final draws are fixed by cup table position — 1st v 4th and 2nd v 3rd for the Knock Out Cup, 5th v 8th and 6th v 7th for the Subsidiary Cup — with the higher-placed team at home in each tie. Knockout matches are a single game, with no second leg.
9.4A player must have played for their team in the league before 31st December that season to be eligible for the remaining cup competition. See the Cups page for full details and the current cup table.

10. Awards & Records

10.1Highest checkout and 180s only count if entered via the Captain Portal. If more than two players are tied on the most 180s, a play-off of 9 darts each is held, with the highest total declared the winner.

11. End of Season

11.1The finals are held in two parts: (1) the Subsidiary Cup and Pairs competition; (2) the Knock Out Cup and Singles competition, plus presentations.
11.2Each team sends 3 representatives for the Singles competition and 3 pairs for the Pairs competition.
